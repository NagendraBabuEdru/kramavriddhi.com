# Skill 4: Forest atmosphere and how Rex reacts to nature

Rex lives in a prehistoric jungle with no people. The world must feel alive, and **Rex's body must respond physically to it**. Everything moves for a reason: wind, water, weight or light.

## Atmosphere layers (back to front)
1. Sky and light: the time of day sets the colour temperature (dawn is pink-gold, noon is neutral, rain is cool blue-grey, sunset is warm orange).
2. Far hills and trees: low contrast and lighter values, tinted toward the sky colour (atmospheric perspective). They barely move.
3. Mid-ground forest: canopy, palms, ferns and cycads (prehistoric plants: tree ferns, cycads, horsetails, conifers and ginkgo; **no grass meadows or flowers dominating**, since grasses and flowers were rare or young).
4. Rex's ground: soil, roots, moss, fallen leaves, pebbles, puddles and shell pieces.
5. Foreground frame elements: big leaves and ferns, darker and possibly blurred, parallaxing fastest.
- Use light shafts through the canopy, dust motes or pollen in the sun, mist low in the morning, and dappled shadow (moving leaf-shadow spots) on Rex when he is under trees.

## Nature in motion
- **Wind** comes as gusts, not a constant push. Gusts travel across the scene, so things farther along the wind direction react later (phase delay). Tall thin things bend more; heavy things barely move. Leaves flutter (fast, small) on top of the sway (slow, big).
- **Rain**: light drops make small splashes and ripples in puddles and drip off leaf tips. In heavy rain the leaves bounce when hit, streams form, the ground darkens with wetness and sheets of water slide off big leaves.
- **Water**: the surface moves in waves and carries floating things up and down. Splashes are crowns of droplets on arcs; ripples are expanding rings that fade. Wet things are shinier (add a specular highlight) and darker.
- **Creatures**: small insects (beetles, dragonflies), birds (or early birds and pterosaurs flying far away), and frogs. They react to Rex: they fly off when he stomps, or come closer when he is still.

## How Rex reacts physically (the "believability list")
| Nature event | Rex's body response |
|---|---|
| Gust of wind | He squints, leans into the wind, the hat tilts or wobbles, the tail swings downwind and he holds his arms close. A strong gust makes him take a stumbling step. |
| Cold | He shivers (a fast, small tremble), hunches with his arms tight to his chest and his tail curled around his feet; his breath can show as a puff. |
| Raindrop on the nose | He blinks hard, his eyes cross toward the nose, a twitch, then a sneeze or a giggle. |
| Heavy rain | He hunches with his head down and squints, then shakes himself dry like a dog: the head first, then the body, then the tail, with a spray of droplets. |
| Puddle or mud | His feet sink a little and make a splash. Mud sticks to his feet (darker feet afterwards), and he leaves footprints. |
| Sun warmth | His eyes half-close happily, he lifts his chin and his body relaxes; he may yawn. |
| Loud thunder | A startle jolt with every part reacting at once, the eyes go wide and the tail stiffens, then he hides or freezes. |
| Slope or uneven ground | His body tilts to keep balance, his arms go out and his tail counterbalances. |
| Falling leaf or insect | His head and eyes track it on its arc (eyes first), and he paws at it. |
| Floating on water | He sways with the raft (overlapping, slightly late), widens his stance for balance and puts his arms out. |

## Physics of Rex himself
- He weighs about as much as a puppy: his feet press down a little, small things move when he bumps them, and leaves crunch when he steps.
- **Contact shadows** are always under his feet; they get darker and smaller when he is close to the ground and lighter and wider when he jumps.
- **Gravity**: falls accelerate. Nothing floats unless it is in water or deliberately slow-motion.
- **Light on Rex**: one key light from the sky or sun direction. The shadow side matches the scene. In forest shade he is cooler and darker; in a sunbeam he is warm, with a rim light.
