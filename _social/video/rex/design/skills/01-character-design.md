# Skill 1: Character design for a baby cartoon dinosaur

Use this when judging or specifying how Rex *looks*: shape, proportion, silhouette and consistency.

## Appeal comes from shape language
- **Circles = friendly, soft, young.** Rex is built from circles and soft pears. Avoid sharp triangles anywhere except tiny, blunt claws and teeth.
- One **big** shape (head), one **medium** (body), several **small** (arms, feet, tail tip, spots). Big–medium–small contrast reads as appealing; equal sizes read as stiff.
- Use **straights against curves**: a slightly flatter belly line against a round back gives rhythm. A blob that is round everywhere has no structure.

## Baby proportions (the "baby schema")
- The head is **40–50 %** of the total standing height. Treat this as the identity rule, never go below 40 %.
- Eyes sit **low on the head**, at or below its vertical middle, set wide apart, and **large** (each eye ≈ 1/4–1/3 of the head width). There is a large forehead/cranium above them.
- Snout: short and rounded, no longer than the eye-to-back-of-head distance. Nostrils are two small dots or commas.
- Limbs: short and thick, with a chubby belly. Little arms are a *T-Rex joke*: keep them tiny, but they must still be able to touch his mouth and wave.
- Tail: thick at the base; its length is about ≤ the body length. It acts as a counterweight and is his most expressive secondary part (like a puppy's).

## Silhouette test
- Fill the character solid black. Every key pose and expression must still read: a raised head = curious, a drooped head and tail = sad, the tail up with the arms out = excited.
- Keep the arms and tail **away from the body outline** in key poses (negative space), or they vanish in silhouette.

## Turnaround and consistency (on-model)
- A model sheet has **front, ¾ front, side (profile), ¾ back and back**, all on shared horizontal guide lines: top of head, eye line, shoulder, belly, knee and ground.
- Volumes must match between views. The head width in the front view equals the head depth in the profile view ± perspective.
- Fixed landmarks to keep in every view: the spot pattern (the same 3–5 spots on the back/head), the belly patch shape, the number of back bumps, the eye shape, and the eggshell hat.
- Line weight is thicker on outer silhouette lines and on the shadow side, and thinner for interior detail. Keep it consistent across views.

## Colour (colour version)
- Limit it to a main green, a darker green for the far side and spots, a cream belly, a soft blush pink, near-black pupils with **two** white highlights, and a warm off-white for the shell hat.
- Value matters more than hue: the face must be the lightest, highest-contrast area of the character, so the eye goes there.
- B&W check: convert to grey. If the belly and the body merge, the values are too close.

## Common failure signs to flag
- "Gecko/lizard" read: head too small, snout too long, body too long and low, eyes on the sides.
- "Mask" read: features drawn on a flat disc, with no volume turn (the eyes don't wrap around the head form).
- "Off-model drift": the eye size or head ratio changes between shots, the spots move, or the hat changes size.
- "Floating": the feet don't plant on the ground plane, there is no contact shadow, or the weight isn't over the feet.
- Tangents: outlines touching exactly (a hand touching the belly outline, the tail tip on the ground line) create visual knots. Overlap or separate them clearly instead.
