# Skill 3: Motion, the 12 principles applied to Rex

Use this when judging movement in test clips or frame strips (several frames of one action side by side).

| Principle | What it means for Rex |
|---|---|
| Squash & stretch | Keep the volume constant. On landing, squash 10–20 % (wider and shorter); on take-off, stretch. The head squashes less than the belly. |
| Anticipation | Before a hop he crouches with the head down and the tail down; before a sneeze, the head tilts back and the nostrils flare. |
| Staging | Face the camera ¾ for emotion and use profile for travel. Don't hide the face behind leaves at key beats. |
| Straight-ahead vs pose-to-pose | Here: pose-to-pose with keyframes and eased in-betweens. |
| Follow-through & overlapping action | The tail lags the body by about 4–8 frames and settles with a small overshoot. The arms flop slightly after a stop, the shell hat wobbles after landing, and the cheeks jiggle. |
| Slow in / slow out | Ease every move, except impacts (which are sudden) and falls (which accelerate). |
| Arcs | The head, the tail tip and hops all travel in arcs, never in straight lines. |
| Secondary action | Tail wags while walking, a sniff while looking, a hand to the mouth when thinking. |
| Timing | A baby moves quickly and lightly. Use 6–8 frames for a small hop and 18–24 for a big one; a head turn takes 6–10 frames. |
| Exaggeration | Push key poses about 20 % beyond "real", especially eyes and mouth in big emotions. |
| Solid drawing | Shapes must have volume: the belly patch wraps around the body and the eyes wrap around the head. |
| Appeal | Readable, charming, simple. |

## Walk/run cycles for a small biped dinosaur
- A walk is 12–16 frames per step at 30 fps (a toddler is bouncy).
  - The body is lowest on the contact/down pose and highest on the passing pose.
  - The head bobs with a slight delay; the tail swings opposite the hips.
- A waddle-run is 8–10 frames per step. The body leans forward, the arms flap, and both feet leave the ground briefly.
- A hop is 3 frames anticipation, then 6–10 in the air on a parabola, then a 2–3 frame squash on landing, then 4–6 settle frames.
- Feet must **stick** to the ground during contact (no sliding). The stride length must match the travel speed.

## Typical motion bugs to flag
- Sliding feet, or floating (no ground contact).
- Moving holds that are too still (dead), or with too much jitter. The pencil-line "boil" must stay subtle on the face.
- Popping: a sudden pose change with no in-between, except for deliberate smears.
- Twinning: both arms or both legs doing exactly the same thing at the same time.
- Volume loss: the head shrinks during a squash, or the body changes size between poses.
