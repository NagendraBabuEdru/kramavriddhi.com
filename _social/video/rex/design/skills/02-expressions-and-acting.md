# Skill 2: Facial expressions and acting (no dialogue)

Rex never speaks. His face, body and little sounds tell everything, so expressions must read in **under half a second**, even on a phone.

## The face kit (each part must move independently)
- **Brows** are the most important emotion signal. Inner ends up = sad or worried; inner ends down = angry or determined; both raised high = surprise; one raised = curious.
- **Upper lids** show energy: wide open = alert or surprised; half-lidded = sleepy, smug or calm; squeezed shut = laughing, crying or effort.
- **Lower lids** pushed up = real (Duchenne) smile or squint; flat = neutral.
- **Pupils**: size (big = love or wonder, tiny = shock), direction (eyes lead the head by 2–4 frames) and highlights (stay fixed relative to the light, never rotating with the eye).
- **Mouth**: corners (up/down), openness and shape (o, wide, wobbly). Show the tongue and teeth only in big emotions.
- **Cheeks** squash up when he smiles big, and the blush appears with joy, shyness or warmth.
- **Snout and nostrils** flare when he sniffs, and wrinkle before a sneeze.

## Core expression set for a baby character (minimum)
Neutral-content, happy, big laugh, giggle (eyes closed), surprised, amazed/wonder, curious (head tilt + one brow), confused, shy/bashful, sad, about-to-cry (lip wobble, wet eyes), crying/wailing, scared, startled (shock: pupils small, body jolts), sleepy/yawn, sneeze (anticipation → release), determined/brave, proud, disgusted (yuck), mischievous, cold/shivering, love/adoring.

## Acting rules
- **Clear staging**: one idea per pose. A strong line of action (a single curve from head through spine to tail) makes poses read.
- **Thought before action**: eyes move → head turns → body follows (overlapping, a few frames apart).
- **Anticipation** for every big change: a small squash or a look away before a jump, a sneeze, a laugh or a cry.
- **Holds** need life: a "moving hold" keeps breathing, blinking, and a small tail or eye drift. Never freeze.
- **Blinks**: about every 2–6 s, irregular, and on head turns (blink on the turn). A blink takes 3–5 frames at 30 fps (close fast, open slower). Use a double blink for surprise or confusion.
- **Contrast**: a change of emotion needs a change of shape: open to closed, up to down, wide to narrow.
- **Babies are reactive**: big, fast changes of feeling, short attention span, curiosity first, fear briefly, then comfort-seeking.

## How to evaluate an expression sheet
1. Cover the body: does the face alone read correctly? Then cover the face: does the body alone read?
2. Are any two expressions too similar (for example, happy vs content)? If so, push one further.
3. Is the volume of the head kept (no flattening when the mouth opens wide)? The jaw opens downward; the head doesn't shrink.
4. Do the tears, blush and sweat drops sit on the head surface and follow it when it tilts?
